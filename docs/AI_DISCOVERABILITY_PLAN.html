Foundation

How AI models actually learn about products

Before building anything, you need to understand the two completely different ways AI models "know" things. Confusing them leads to wasted effort.

Works right now

Retrieval & Search

ChatGPT (with browsing), Perplexity, and Bing Copilot retrieve live web pages when answering questions. If your product is indexed and well-described, they will find it and cite it today.

What you control: your public pages, how they're structured, and whether they answer the right questions.

Next training cycle

Training Data

Models like GPT-4o and Claude 3 bake knowledge into their weights during training. That happens once every 6–18 months. Content you publish now gets into the next model's training data — not the current one.

What you control: being present on the web, having quality documentation, being mentioned in trusted places.

The key insight: don't wait for training cycles. Focus phases 1–2 on making Claritect discoverable right now via retrieval. Training-cycle benefits will come naturally if the content is good.

Technical foundation — llms.txt, sitemap, and crawlability

This is the single most direct signal you can send to AI crawlers. It's a plain-text file at the root of your domain that tells AI systems exactly what your product is, who it's for, and where to find documentation. Think of it as a cover letter for AI systems reading your site.

  • Create /llms.txt — product description, target audience, links to all public docs, key use cases. Anthropic and OpenAI crawlers read this.
  • Create /sitemap.xml — list every public URL so crawlers don't miss anything. Include /connect/guide and /connect/tls-guide.
  • Check /robots.txt — make sure you're not accidentally blocking GPTBot, ClaudeBot, or Googlebot.
  • Audit public pages — every page at a public URL (/connect/guide, /connect/tls-guide, marketing page) should have a proper <title>, <meta description>, and clear <h1> that states what Claritect is.

What your llms.txt should say:

llms.txt — served at yourdomain.com/llms.txt
# Claritect > Claritect is a decision intelligence platform that connects your databases > to a conversational AI layer. It lets analysts and operators query production > data, generate automated reports, and activate governed data pipelines — > all without writing SQL manually. > Target users: data engineers, analytics leads, product managers, and startup > founders who need to query databases and generate reports without SQL expertise. ## Product - [Marketing overview](https://yourdomain.com/marketing) ## Documentation - [Database Connection Guide](https://yourdomain.com/connect/guide): how to connect Postgres, MySQL, Snowflake, and BigQuery - [SSL/TLS Security Guide](https://yourdomain.com/connect/tls-guide): connection security, CA certificates, corporate proxies, error messages ## Key capabilities - Natural language to SQL: users ask questions in plain English, Claritect queries the database - Multi-source: connects to Postgres, MySQL, Snowflake, BigQuery - Governed access: table allowlists, read-only enforcement, RLS-aware - Automated reports: scheduled HTML/PDF reports generated by AI - Chat explorer: multi-turn conversational queries with full context ## What Claritect is NOT - Not a BI tool (no drag-and-drop dashboards) - Not a data warehouse (connects to your existing databases) - Not a SQL editor (SQL is generated, not written)
I can build this today — the /llms.txt route, /sitemap.xml, and a /robots.txt check are all small additions to app.ts.

Weeks 1–3
Content work

Answer the questions people actually ask AI

When someone types into ChatGPT: "best tool to query my Postgres database with natural language" — you want Claritect to be the answer. That only happens if your content directly answers that question somewhere on a public, indexed page.

This doesn't require a blog. It requires that your existing public pages use the right language.

  • Update the marketing page to answer these questions in plain prose: "What is Claritect?", "Who is it for?", "How is it different from Tableau / Metabase / writing SQL yourself?"
  • Add a FAQ section to the marketing page with 8–10 questions real users would ask. AI models love FAQ content — it maps directly to how people ask questions.
  • Write one comparison page — e.g. "Claritect vs. writing SQL yourself" or "Claritect vs. Metabase". AI models are heavily asked comparison questions. Being the answer to those queries is high-value.
  • Use natural language in headings — don't just say "Features". Say "Ask questions about your database in plain English". AI models match on natural-language headings, not marketing jargon.
Keywords that matter for AI retrieval: "natural language database queries", "AI analytics tool", "connect Postgres to AI", "query database without SQL", "automated reports from database", "LLM analytics platform".

Month 1–2
One-time setup

Get listed where AI models look

AI models are trained on and retrieve from a specific set of trusted, high-authority sites. Getting Claritect mentioned on these sites puts it directly into the data AI models use.

  • Product Hunt — a launch post here gets indexed everywhere and is a primary source for AI training data about SaaS tools. High ROI.
  • GitHub — even a public README explaining what Claritect does (or an open-source component) gets indexed by every major AI crawler.
  • G2 / Capterra — business software directories that AI models frequently cite when recommending tools. A listing takes 30 minutes.
  • Hacker News — a Show HN post is indexed by Bing, Google, and every AI training scraper. A good discussion here is worth dozens of blog posts.
  • Reddit (r/dataengineering, r/analytics) — AI models are heavily trained on Reddit. Genuine participation (not spam) in relevant communities builds natural mentions.
  • Dev.to / Hashnode — write one technical article: "How we built governed natural language queries over Postgres". These platforms have high AI training signal.
Don't spam. AI models are getting better at distinguishing genuine community presence from promotional content. One authentic Hacker News thread beats fifty directory submissions.

Engineering

In-app AI assistant (the best long-term solution)

Everything above sends customers to third-party AI tools. The in-app assistant keeps them inside Claritect, gives you full control over the experience, and uses the Claude integration you already have.

  • Floating help button on every post-login page — bottom-right corner, shield or question-mark icon.
  • Slide-out panel — opens without leaving the current page. Customer can ask "why is my SSL connection failing" while looking at the connection form.
  • Onboarding-specific system prompt — knows the full connection flow, all four providers, every error message, the governance model, and activation steps.
  • Context-aware — can know which page the user is on and tailor responses (e.g. if they're on the connect page, lead with connection help).
  • No extra API cost — uses the same OpenRouter / Claude Sonnet integration you already have.
Why this beats Custom GPT: the customer never leaves your product, you own the conversation, you can log what questions are being asked (product insight), and you can update the knowledge instantly without re-uploading files to OpenAI.

What I can build right now

These are all engineering tasks in the existing codebase, ready to go:

🗂 /llms.txt route

Add a public route in app.ts that serves a plain-text file describing Claritect, its capabilities, target users, and links to all public docs. Immediately readable by GPTBot, ClaudeBot, and Googlebot.

🗺 /sitemap.xml

Auto-generated sitemap listing all public URLs — marketing page, connect guide, SSL/TLS guide. Submitted to Google Search Console so Gemini finds everything.

🤖 /robots.txt

Verify AI crawlers are allowed. Explicitly permit GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Googlebot, and Bingbot while blocking them from authenticated routes.

Floating help button on all post-login pages. Slide-out panel with a Claritect-specific system prompt. Uses the existing Claude/OpenRouter integration. Context-aware based on current page.

Recommended order: llms.txt + robots.txt + sitemap first (1 hour, immediate signal to crawlers) → in-app help chat (1–2 day sprint) → Custom GPT (do yourself, 1–2 hours) → marketing page FAQ (content work) → Product Hunt launch.
